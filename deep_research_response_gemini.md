# **Architectural Paradigms, Augmentation Regimes, and Pretrained Encoders for Small-Scale Singer Identification on Artist20**

Singer identification (SID) in polyphonic music presents a challenging music information retrieval (MIR) problem characterized by severe confounding variables1. In small-scale benchmarks such as the Artist20 dataset—which comprises 20 artists across approximately 950 training tracks split strictly at the album level—models are exceptionally prone to learning album-specific production signatures, instrumentation, studio acoustic profiles, and mastering equalization rather than the intrinsic vocal timbre of the artist2. When evaluation is conducted on held-out test albums, classifiers relying on these non-vocal shortcuts experience severe generalization performance degradation3.  
Systematic progress requires evaluating advanced deep learning paradigms, targeted data augmentation recipes, and specialized self-supervised learning (SSL) audio representations. This report delivers an evaluation of candidate architectures beyond standard Convolutional Recurrent Neural Networks (CRNN) and non-local self-attention, assesses regularizers engineered to decouple vocal identity from production confounds, and surveys open-access audio encoder checkpoints specifically suited for singing voice understanding4.

## **Architectural Candidates Beyond Standard CRNNs**

Baseline evaluations on Artist20 typically rely on classical machine learning with hand-crafted features, frame-level CNNs, CRNNs (e.g., Choi et al. baselines or GRU temporal pooling), and Fully Generalized Non-Local Networks (FGNL)1. Recent MIR and audio classification literature (2023–2026) offers alternative modeling paradigms. For small-scale datasets with album-level holds, ideal architectures must balance high representation capacity with inductive biases that prevent over-parameterized memorization of background accompaniment4.

### **Self-Supervised Singer-Identity Encoders**

Self-supervised models explicitly optimized for vocal timbre invariance offer a strong departure from general music classification backbones9. Pioneered by Torres et al. (ISMIR 2023), the ssl-singer-identity framework utilizes an EfficientNet-B0 backbone paired with non-contrastive and contrastive self-supervised loss functions, including Bootstrap Your Own Latent (BYOL), Variance-Covariance-Regularization (VICReg), and Decoupled Contrastive Learning11. Trained on large collections of isolated vocal tracks, these encoders operate at a 44.1 kHz sampling rate to preserve high-frequency vocal harmonics and formants often discarded in standard 16 kHz speech processing11.  
The primary structural innovation lies in applying heavy pitch-shifting and time-stretching augmentations during the self-supervised phase, forcing the network to map varied musical performances by the same vocalist to a unified, pitch-invariant latent space9. When adapted to Artist20, these encoders serve as non-linear feature extractors that remain robust to instrumentation because they were pre-trained exclusively on isolated singing voices9.

### **Distilled Lightweight Convolutional Networks**

Although Vision Transformers (ViTs) and Audio Spectrogram Transformers (AST) achieve state-of-the-art results on massive datasets like AudioSet, their lack of translational inductive biases frequently leads to severe overfitting on small datasets (\~1000 tracks)8. The EfficientAT framework developed by Schmid et al. (ICASSP 2023 / IEEE TASLP 2024\) addresses this limitation through Transformer-to-CNN knowledge distillation8. Lightweight MobileNetV3 and Dynamic MobileNet (DyMN) architectures are trained under the guidance of performant Transformer ensembles (such as PaSST)8.  
These distilled CNNs retain strong local receptive field inductive biases while embedding global contextual knowledge captured by Transformers8. Models such as mn10\_as and dymn20\_as offer parameter footprints under 5 million parameters and low multiply-accumulate (MAC) counts, providing high representation capacity without the parameter inflation that leads to album-level memorization8.

### **Mid-Level Perceptual Feature Fusion Networks**

Rather than relying solely on low-level Mel-spectrograms—which readily encode studio mixing traits—recent studies propose fusing acoustic timbral embeddings (e.g., speaker X-vectors) with mid-level perceptual musical features2. Mid-level features quantify perceptual musical qualities including melodiousness, articulation, rhythmic complexity, rhythmic stability, dissonance, tonal stability, and modality4.  
Fusing frame-level timbral representations with mid-level perceptual matrices in a multi-stream CRNN or KNN-Net architecture forces the model to evaluate the vocal line relative to high-level musical context3. By explicitly accounting for tonal and harmonic stability, the network decouples the vocalist's timbral signature from the background arrangement, preventing the model from utilizing specific guitar or synthesizer timbres as class proxies4.

### **Pitch-Independent Adversarial Latent Encoders**

Originating from neural singing voice conversion and beautification systems (e.g., Neurodyne and HFC frameworks), adversarial representation learning offers a method to isolate vocal identity from musical content15. These architectures utilize a dual-head design comprising an encoder, a latent projection space, a primary classifier, and an adversarial pitch/content predictor15.  
During training, the encoder is optimized to maximize singer identification accuracy while simultaneously minimizing the ability of the adversarial pitch predictor to reconstruct fundamental frequency (![][image1]) contours or musical key from the latent embedding15. This minimax optimization strips pitch, melody, and key dependencies from the latent representation, leaving a clean timbral vector that is resistant to album-specific key or register bias15.

## **Augmentation and Regularization Strategies for Album-Correlated Datasets**

In the Artist20 dataset, album-level splits enforce that no tracks from the validation or test albums appear in the training split3. Because songs within the same album share identical recording studios, mixing engineers, microphonic setups, instrument configurations, and mastering chains, standard supervised models tend to optimize for album identity rather than singer identity3. To counter this, empirical strategies must be employed to break the statistical correlation between vocal timbre and background production16.

### **Vocal Source Separation as a Preprocessing Step**

Isolating the singing voice via deep learning source separation—such as Demucs V4, Wave-U-Net, or Robust Principal Component Analysis (RPCA)—is an effective strategy for eliminating accompaniment confounds3.  
Literature systematically demonstrates that evaluating singer identification on raw polyphonic mixtures yields accuracy baselines in the range of 56% to 60% on Artist20 under strict album-level splits1. Introducing vocal source separation increases classification accuracy to 80%–85%, with specialized attention-CRNN and KNN-Net architectures achieving up to 81%–93.5% accuracy2. Eliminating the accompaniment stem removes the non-vocal spectral energy that models otherwise exploit as shortcut features18.  
However, source separation introduces predictable processing artifacts. Demucs and Hybrid Spectrogram-U-Net models can create phase distortion, musical noise (gargling artifacts in transient regions), vocal cancellation during dense polyphonic climaxes, and high-frequency roll-off. Furthermore, when vocal separation algorithms fail, they often leave residual bleed from instruments sharing the vocal frequency band (e.g., electric guitars, saxophones). If a classifier learns to identify a singer based on the unique spectral profile of these residual artifacts, the confound is merely shifted from the clean instrument to the artifact signature.

### **Disentangling Standard vs. Confound-Targeting Augmentations**

Standard audio data augmentations impact model regularization differently. They can be divided into generic acoustic regularizers and targeted confound-decoupling operations:

* **Generic Regularizers**:  
  * *SpecAugment (Frequency/Time Masking)*: Prevents memorization of specific time-frequency blocks but does not alter the underlying spectral envelope or background instrumental timbre.  
  * *Time Stretching*: Alters tempo without changing pitch; useful for duration invariance but ineffective at removing production or mastering signatures.  
* **Confound-Targeting Augmentations**:  
  * *Pitch Shifting (Without Formant Correction)*: Shifts the fundamental frequency (![][image1]) along with formant locations. This forces the model to ignore absolute pitch registers and focus on relative harmonic ratios.  
  * *Formant Shifting (Pitch-Preserving)*: Modifies the simulated vocal tract length while maintaining ![][image1]. This explicitly expands the vocal identity space, preventing the model from overfitting to a singer's comfortable dynamic pitch range.  
  * *Production/EQ Jittering*: Applying randomized parametric equalization filters (notch, high-pass, low-pass, peaking filters) and dynamic range compression directly mimics variation in studio mastering setups across different albums.  
  * *Additive Background Noise Injection*: Superimposing non-stationary background noise, room impulse responses (reverberation), or instrumental accompaniment stems from unrelated genres forces the classifier to attend to invariant, high-energy vocal formants.

### **Singer-ID Specific Augmentation Recipes: Pitch-Aware Remixing and Vocal Swapping**

Advanced augmentation protocols directly target the joint distribution ![][image2] by synthetically reconstructing polyphonic tracks17.

* **Chromagram-Based Pitch-Aware Remixing**: Originally proposed by Wang et al. and Yuan et al. (ICASSP 2022), this technique extracts isolated vocal stems and accompaniment stems from the dataset17. For a given vocal segment, the algorithm calculates a 12-dimensional pitch-class chromagram22. It then searches a pool of accompaniment stems from *different* artists and albums to find segments with high cross-correlation in chroma space22. Mixing the vocal stem with a pitch-aligned accompaniment stem from a foreign album produces a harmonically coherent synthetic song17.  
* **Vocal Swapping across Album Boundaries**: By forcing artist ![][image3]'s vocal track to be paired with artist ![][image4]'s background instrumentation (from an entirely different album), the conditional probability linking vocal timbre to studio mix settings is broken. During training, the loss function enforces that the target label remains artist ![][image3] regardless of whether the backing track originates from album ![][image3], album ![][image4], or an external multitrack repository such as MUSDB1822.

## **Pretrained SSL and Supervised Audio Checkpoints for Singing Voice**

Leveraging pretrained audio encoders via linear probing or fine-tuning provides an effective defense against small-sample overfitting7. However, the choice of pretraining domain (speech vs. general music vs. singing voice) heavily impacts downstream performance on singer identification7.

### **Dedicated Singing-Voice SSL Models**

Models trained explicitly on singing voice corpora represent the most domain-aligned representations for singer identification9.  
The ssl-singer-identity repository (Torres et al., ISMIR 2023\) provides a suite of models hosted on HuggingFace, including byol, contrastive, contrastive-vc, vicreg, and uniformity checkpoints11. Pretrained on large collections of isolated vocal tracks at 44.1 kHz, these models utilize self-supervised objective functions designed to eliminate pitch and content dependencies while maximizing singer identity clustering11. The byol and vicreg checkpoints demonstrate high out-of-domain generalization, outperforming speech-trained baselines and general audio representations on singer verification and identification tasks9. The repository and weights are open (MIT License) and accessible without gating11.

### **General Music SSL Models**

General music representations capture holistic musical structures, including pitch, harmony, rhythm, and instrumentation7.  
MERT (m-a-p/MERT-v1-95M and MERT-v1-330M) is trained on large music datasets using a combination of Masked Language Modeling (MLM), Residual Vector Quantization (RVQ-VAE) discrete tokens, and Constant-Q Transform (CQT) acoustic features7. While MERT exhibits strong performance across general MIR tasks (such as tagging, key detection, and genre classification), its linear probe performance on singer identification can be affected by its retention of background instrumental information7. When probed with raw polyphonic audio, MERT embeddings encode both the singer's voice and the background arrangement. Similarly, MusicHuBERT and Music2Vec adapt speech SSL architectures to polyphonic music data7. They are openly accessible on HuggingFace without gating, but require downstream fine-tuning or vocal separation preprocessing to prevent accompaniment features from dominating the classification decision7.

### **Speech Speaker Verification Models**

Pretrained speaker verification models trained on speech corpora (such as VoxCeleb or VoxBlink) transfer effectively to singing voice identification due to shared acoustic properties of the human vocal tract7.  
ECAPA-TDNN (speechbrain/spkrec-ecapa-voxceleb) employs 1D Squeeze-and-Excitation Time-Delay Neural Networks to capture speaker-specific vocal tract characteristics7. Despite the domain gap between speech and singing (singing features extended pitch ranges, vibrato, and sustained vowels), ECAPA-TDNN embeddings capture fundamental formant ratios that remain invariant during singing7. Advanced speaker verification backbones published in 2023–2024 include CAM++ and ERes2NetV27. CAM++ incorporates context-aware masking to focus on stable vocal frames, while ERes2NetV2 utilizes dual-stage local and global feature fusion7. These checkpoints are available via ModelScope and HuggingFace, offering linear probing performance when applied to isolated vocal stems7.

## **Experimental Roadmap and Ablation Design**

To maximize the impact of the ablation study on Artist20 without disrupting active training runs, the following experimental sequence integrates these findings into a unified pipeline.  
First, front-end input variations should be systematically isolated by evaluating all candidate backbones under two input conditions: raw polyphonic audio mixtures and Demucs V4 isolated vocal stems18. Tracking the accuracy delta between these conditions quantifies each model's reliance on background album confounds18.  
Second, the SonyCSLParis/ssl-singer-identity BYOL and VICReg checkpoints should be incorporated into the evaluation suite11. Because these encoders were pre-trained explicitly for pitch- and content-invariant vocal identity on 44.1 kHz isolated singing voices, they provide an aligned benchmark for linear probing and fine-tuning experiments9.  
Third, lightweight distilled models from fschmid56/EfficientAT (mn10\_as or dymn20\_as) should be evaluated as fine-tunable backbones8. Their compact parameterization and local convolutional inductive biases offer a structurally distinct alternative to standard CRNNs and unconstrained ViTs8.  
Finally, for data augmentation ablations, chromagram-based pitch-aware remixing should be implemented across album splits17. Re-mixing vocal stems over pitch-matched background tracks from held-out training albums provides a rigorous test for evaluating whether the model relies on vocal identity or album-correlated background production22.

## **Conclusion**

Overcoming production and album-level confounds in small-scale singer identification datasets like Artist20 requires aligning model architecture, data augmentation, and pretrained representations3. While standard CRNNs trained on raw polyphonic audio tend to overfit to background production, combining vocal source separation with pitch-aware remixing effectively breaks album shortcuts17. Furthermore, leveraging specialized singing-voice SSL representations (ssl-singer-identity) or distilled lightweight CNNs (EfficientAT) provides high representation capacity while maintaining robustness against out-of-album acoustic distribution shifts8. Integrating these architectural paradigms and augmentation recipes into downstream ablations will enable a robust, vocal-centric evaluation of singer classification performance.
